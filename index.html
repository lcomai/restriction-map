Restriction mapping lab

Estimate the intact DNA length, choose digests, then reconstruct the hidden restriction map. Each digest uses a fresh aliquot of the same DNA.

Practice 1 · bank example

1. Run and measure

Choose enzymes and add digest lanes before loading. The ladder and uncut sample are included automatically. After stopping, move over the gel to read an approximate size; click to hold the measuring line. Ladder labels are in kb.

Move over a band to estimate its size.
Complete digests. Uncut circular forms: OC = open circle, SC = supercoiled. Their sizes cannot be reliably determined by comparing their mobility with the ladder, which consists of linear DNA fragments. Smaller fragments travel farther. Equal-sized fragments co-migrate; brighter bands can contain more DNA. Tracking dyes: cyan (slower), violet (faster); hidden when stopped. Bands that pass the bottom are lost. Time is illustrative, not laboratory minutes.

2. Build your map

Click the bar to add the selected enzyme. Drag sites to position them, or edit their kb positions below. Focus a site and use arrow keys to move it by 0.1 kb.

How this prototype works

Unknown mode randomly chooses a linear or circular molecule without labeling its shape or circular bands. Select your inferred shape in the answer section; changing this answer preserves the gel and your site coordinates. Ruler values are apparent sizes relative to a linear ladder, not reliable molecular lengths for circular conformations. Reveal actual map discloses the hidden shape. The band simulation remains idealized.

Circular DNA: OC and SC bands use fixed illustrative apparent-size factors, not predicted physical mobilities. Do not size these forms with a linear ladder. One cut linearizes the circle; each additional cut adds a fragment. No-cut digests retain both circular forms. The circular example uses the same site coordinates as the starting linear example, but is a new teaching example, not the original bank question. Changing DNA shape or problem type starts a new problem and clears the gel and entered map. The bank example selects three cuts. Circular maps are checked by their predicted digest bands, so rotation and reversal are naturally equivalent.

Positions and fragment sizes are in kilobases (kb). The gel uses a logarithmic migration scale from 20 to 0.2 kb. Animated mode loads all selected lanes together and advances them under power. Stop hides the two illustrative tracking dyes; resume continues from the same positions. Rerun same gel restarts the same loaded samples from zero time, restoring even fragments that ran off the gel; this is a simulation replay. Prepare fresh gel retains your chosen lanes and map but replaces all samples, including any lost off the bottom. The ruler recalibrates to elapsed run time. Very short runs have poor separation. Switching modes resets the run. The ruler reports approximate size, not the exact fragment value. Map checking compares predicted and observed visible band positions only in the current gel, with a 4-pixel tolerance in the gel coordinate system. Every visible band must have a counterpart; co-migrating fragment multiplicity and brightness are not graded. Bands lost off the gel supply no size constraint. Circular forms indicate no cuts but are not sized. A consistent map can differ from the hidden map. The non-uniqueness button records a student claim, not a verified uniqueness result. Neither practice level is guaranteed unique.
